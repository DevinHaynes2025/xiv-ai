## Founder user story

As the founder of XIV AI, I want XIV to become a governed **enterprise operating intelligence layer** that connects authorized business systems, continuously reconstructs the state of an organization, detects operational problems and opportunities, creates temporary specialist agent teams when needed, simulates possible interventions, explains recommendations to executives, executes only explicitly authorized actions, and learns from measured outcomes — so XIV functions as a **Business Hospital** for organizations without replacing human ownership, bypassing enterprise controls, or becoming an unrestricted autonomous operator.

## Architecture contracts (story §§1–102)

### 1. Compact enterprise architecture

Human executives sit above Executive Command, which sits above Guardian, which sits above the XIV Enterprise Control Plane. Systems (ERP/CRM/WMS/TMS/Finance/HRIS/BI/Documents) feed the Business Digital Twin; the Business Hospital Engine diagnoses, simulates, and forecasts; task forces produce recommendations; humans decide; authorized workflows execute; outcomes feed learning. Every arrow is a governed hop, not an implicit trust edge.

### 2. XEOS — XIV Enterprise Operating System

**XEOS** is a governed intelligence and coordination layer **above** existing enterprise systems. It does not replace ERP, CRM, WMS, TMS, HRIS, or finance systems and does not become the system of record. Responsibilities: system integration, enterprise state reconstruction, cross-functional reasoning, workflow orchestration, decision support, simulation, evidence, human approval, outcome learning.

### 3. Enterprise System Registry

Document-only registry `xiv_enterprise_systems` with: `system_id`, `organization_id`, `universe_id`, `system_type`, `vendor`, `instance_name`, `capabilities`, `read_scope`, `write_scope`, `classification_ceiling`, `authentication_state`, `health_state`, `last_sync`, `status`. Registry rows are declarations under RLS, not live sessions.

### 4. Supported enterprise domains

Initial classes: ERP · CRM · WMS · TMS · SCM · FINANCE · PROCUREMENT · HRIS · MANUFACTURING · ECOMMERCE · POS · DATA_WAREHOUSE · DATA_LAKE · BI · DOCUMENTS · COLLABORATION · TICKETING. **Category support ≠ connection.** No system is considered connected merely because XIV names its category.

### 5. Enterprise connection states

`DISCOVERED → EVALUATING → CONFIGURED → SANDBOX → VERIFIED → AUTHORIZED → AVAILABLE`, plus `DEGRADED`, `SUSPENDED`, `REVOKED`. Only **AVAILABLE** systems may participate in authorized workflows. Unknown or stale state resolves to unavailable, never to available.

### 6. XBDT — Business Digital Twin

A structured representation of the enterprise's current operational state: customers, suppliers, inventory, orders, shipments, warehouses, facilities, employees, teams, machines, products, cash flow, budgets, projects, contracts, risks, workflows, service levels. **It is not a database dump** and it is not an entitlement — twin presence never implies permission to read or write the source.

### 7. Twin entity graph

The twin models relationships, not isolated records: `CUSTOMER → ORDER → PRODUCT → INVENTORY → WAREHOUSE → SHIPMENT → CARRIER` and `SUPPLIER → PURCHASE ORDER → MATERIAL → FACTORY → PRODUCT`. Edges carry source, classification, and confidence.

### 8. Temporal state

Every important business object carries `current_state`, `previous_state`, `effective_at`, `observed_at`, `expected_state`, `forecast_state`. This lets XIV answer **what changed**, not only **what exists**. Forecast state is always labeled as forecast.

### 9. Business Event Mesh

Enterprise systems emit normalized events (`inventory.low`, `shipment.delayed`, `supplier.failed`, `order.cancelled`, `payment.overdue`, `customer.churn_risk`, `machine.degraded`, `employee_capacity.low`, `budget.threshold`, `security.incident`) into the 62H Event Mesh. Event delivery is not authority (compose 62E/62H): routing an event never expands rights.

### 10. Business state reconstruction

`EVENTS + SYSTEM SNAPSHOTS + HISTORICAL STATE + HUMAN CONTEXT → XIV BUSINESS STATE`. Reconstruction **must record uncertainty** where data is incomplete, including which systems were unavailable and which fields are stale.

### 11. XBHE — Business Hospital Engine

Workflow: SYMPTOM → DIAGNOSIS → ROOT CAUSE → SEVERITY → TREATMENT OPTIONS → SIMULATION → RECOMMENDATION → HUMAN DECISION → TREATMENT → MONITORING → OUTCOME. The engine may complete every step up to HUMAN DECISION autonomously; it may not cross it.

### 12. Business symptoms

Examples in scope: inventory stockout risk, late shipments, supplier instability, declining margins, customer churn, warehouse congestion, labor shortage, forecast error, excess inventory, cash-flow pressure, production downtime, software incident.

### 13. Symptom Detector

Inputs: metrics, events, forecasts, thresholds, anomaly models, human reports, historical comparisons. Output: `symptom_id`, `severity`, `confidence`, affected domain, affected value, evidence, recommended investigation. Low-confidence detections are surfaced as low-confidence, not rounded up.

### 14. No automatic diagnosis from one signal

`LATE ORDERS` must not become `WAREHOUSE FAILURE` without evidence. Competing causes (supplier delay, warehouse backlog, carrier delay, system error, demand spike, staff shortage, bad forecast) must each be considered and explicitly ruled in or out.

### 15. Diagnostic task force

Dynamic team drawn from Operations, Supply Chain, Finance, Data Analyst, Customer, Risk, and Skeptic agents — **only roles relevant to the symptom activate**. Teams are minted through the 62I Foundry under the 62E population governor with charter, budget, expiry, and kill switch.

### 16. Diagnostic workflow

SYMPTOM → COLLECT EVIDENCE → CREATE HYPOTHESES → TEST HYPOTHESES → RULE OUT CAUSES → ESTIMATE IMPACT → ROOT-CAUSE RANKING. Ranking must retain the rejected hypotheses and why they were rejected.

### 17. Root cause graph

Causes form a graph, not a single string:

```
CUSTOMER ORDERS LATE
       │
       ├── warehouse congestion
       │      ↓
       │   labor shortage
       │
       ├── carrier delay
       │
       └── supplier delay
```

**Each edge carries evidence.** Unsupported edges are drawn as hypotheses, not findings.

### 18. Evidence strength

Every diagnosis distinguishes observed fact · inference · prediction · assumption · human opinion · unknown. **Do not blur them.** A prediction rendered as a fact is a park-level honesty failure.

### 19. Treatment Planner

Candidate treatments: rebalance inventory, change reorder point, reroute shipment, increase temporary labor, change carrier mix, adjust supplier allocation, change production schedule, modify workflow, change pricing, pause promotion. **All remain proposals until authorized.**

### 20. Treatment restrictions

Agents may recommend. Consequential execution is separately controlled. Explicit authorization is required for supplier cancellation, contract change, pricing change, employee action, major financial commitment, production shutdown, customer communication, and external purchase.

### 21. Business Simulation Engine

Enterprise-focused reuse of 62H/62I simulation. Input: current state, proposed action, assumptions. Output: expected impact, upside, downside, cost, risk, time, **uncertainty**. Simulation output is labeled SIMULATED and never presented as a measured outcome.

### 22. Treatment comparison

Options (e.g. reroute inventory / expedite shipment / increase production / do nothing) are compared on customer impact, cost, service level, margin, risk, time, and operational feasibility. Single-metric selection is not permitted for consequential recommendations.

### 23. Counterfactual analysis

**"Do nothing" is a mandatory baseline.** A recommendation must beat the do-nothing case on relevant business metrics, with the comparison shown.

### 24. Executive Recommendation Contract

Every consequential recommendation contains: problem, business impact, root-cause hypothesis, evidence, options, recommended option, expected outcome, cost, risk, uncertainty, required approvals, next checkpoint. A recommendation missing any field is incomplete, not "approved by default."

### 25. XEC — XIV Executive Command

The executive surface answers: what is happening · why · what will happen next · what should we do · what will it cost · what is the risk · who needs to approve · what happened after the last decision. Command surface visibility is observation, not authority to bypass gates (compose 62H).

### 26. Morning Executive Brief

A bounded daily package (critical issues, recommended actions, items requiring approval). **All numbers must come from measured or model outputs** with provenance; no illustrative figures may appear as live metrics.

### 27. Executive storytelling

Instead of `inventory = 12,402`, XIV explains the movement, the driver, and the projection, with each claim traceable to a source. This supports the XIV *data tells a story* philosophy — narrative must not add confidence the evidence does not carry.

### 28. Business story timeline

A causal timeline (supplier delayed → safety stock breached → transfer requested → service level declined → recommendation) lets executives see causality over time. Timeline links to the same evidence as the root cause graph.

### 29. Enterprise Workflow OS

The 62H workflow engine becomes operational for: Supplier Risk Response, Inventory Rebalancing, Late Shipment Investigation, Customer Escalation, Security Incident, Budget Variance Review, Maintenance Incident.

### 30. Workflow authority levels

Each step is classified `READ` · `ANALYZE` · `RECOMMEND` · `PREPARE_ACTION` · `REQUEST_APPROVAL` · `EXECUTE_BOUNDED_ACTION` · `CONSEQUENTIAL_ACTION`, each with separate permissions. A workflow cannot promote its own step class.

### 31. Read before write

Default enterprise adoption phases: PHASE 1 READ → PHASE 2 ANALYZE → PHASE 3 RECOMMEND → PHASE 4 approved limited WRITE. **Do not start enterprise onboarding with unrestricted write access.**

### 32. XEAG — Enterprise Action Gateway

Every approved write flows WORKFLOW → ACTION REQUEST → GUARDIAN → HUMAN APPROVAL IF REQUIRED → XEAG → CONNECTOR → TARGET SYSTEM. There is no path from an agent to a target system that bypasses the gateway, and no path that hands an agent a raw credential.

### 33. Action contract

`action_id`, `organization_id`, `universe_id`, `system_id`, `actor`, `purpose`, `operation`, `target`, `before_state`, `proposed_change`, `classification`, `approval`, `budget`, `idempotency_key`, `status`. `before_state` capture is required so outcomes and rollbacks are reconstructable.

### 34. Idempotent enterprise actions

Where technically possible, actions carry an `idempotency_key` to reduce duplicate execution. Where the target system cannot support idempotency, that limitation is recorded on the action rather than assumed away.

### 35. Consequential action classes

Payment, purchase order, pricing, contract, employee action, production shutdown, inventory write-off, customer refund, external communication. **Default: HUMAN APPROVAL REQUIRED.**

### 36. Autonomous low-risk actions

An organization may explicitly authorize narrow operations (refresh dashboard, create internal ticket, update non-sensitive workflow status, schedule approved internal task, request fresh data). **Only explicitly configured actions qualify** — there is no "low risk by inference" category.

### 37. Autonomous operations boundary

62K does **not** mean AI runs the company without humans. It means AI monitors, analyzes, coordinates, simulates, recommends, and executes **only within defined authority**.

### 38. Human executive roles

Governance roles may include CEO, COO, CFO, CIO, CTO, CISO, supply chain leader, operations leader, business unit owner. Role permissions are organization-defined; XIV does not ship implicit executive authority.

### 39. Executive approval routing

Approvals route by organization policy (operational adjustment → operations manager; large expenditure → CFO policy; sensitive security change → CISO; material contract → designated business/legal authority). Routing rules are data, not agent judgment.

### 40. Executive delegation

Every delegation records scope, amount/risk ceiling where applicable, operations, duration, organization, Universe, approver, and expiry. **No vague permanent delegation.**

### 41. Financial boundary

Finance agents may analyze, forecast, model, compare, recommend. They may **not** autonomously send payment, open a bank account, borrow funds, trade assets, sign financing, or change banking credentials. `AUTO_FINANCIAL_COMMITMENT=false`.

### 42. HR boundary

HR agents may analyze workforce capacity, identify skill gaps, prepare staffing scenarios, and summarize feedback. They may **not** autonomously hire, fire, discipline, or change compensation. `AUTO_HIRING_DECISION=false` / `AUTO_TERMINATION_DECISION=false`.

### 43. Procurement boundary

Procurement agents may find shortages, compare approved suppliers, estimate costs, and prepare purchase requests. They may **not** sign contracts or commit spend. `AUTO_CONTRACT_EXECUTION=false`.

### 44. Customer experience agent

May analyze support tickets, customer sentiment, service levels, churn signals, and product feedback, and recommend interventions. Customer-facing communication remains a consequential action (§35).

### 45. Anonymous employee intelligence

Where organization policy permits: EMPLOYEE FEEDBACK → ANONYMIZATION → CLASSIFICATION → AGGREGATE ANALYSIS → EXECUTIVE INSIGHT. **Never expose identities through supposedly anonymous analytics**, including through small-cohort inference.

### 46. Employee signal guardrails

Require minimum aggregation thresholds, privacy controls, a no-retaliation workflow, restricted access, and audit. Exact organizational and legal policies require human governance — XIV does not infer them.

### 47. Consumer intelligence

Future consumer-facing XIV may contribute authorized product feedback, reviews, innovation proposals, demand signals, and feature votes. Enterprise systems may consume **aggregate** intelligence under policy.

### 48. Product innovation loop

CUSTOMER IDEA → CLASSIFY → GROUP SIMILAR IDEAS → MARKET ANALYSIS → TECHNICAL FEASIBILITY → FINANCIAL MODEL → SIMULATION → BUSINESS REVIEW. Output is a reviewed proposal, not a roadmap commitment.

### 49. Enterprise agent departments

Logical groups may include Executive Intelligence, Finance, Supply Chain, Operations, Sales, Marketing, Customer Experience, IT, Cybersecurity, HR, Procurement, legal-support analysis, Innovation, Sustainability. **Agents remain capability-based, not unrestricted virtual employees.**

### 50. Cross-department task forces

Cross-functional problems (e.g. falling margin) activate finance, supply chain, pricing, sales, operations, and skeptic roles together rather than being solved in departmental silos. Composition is bounded by the same governor as §15.

### 51. XIV Virtual AI Board

A bounded advisory set (strategy, finance, operations, technology, security, customer, risk advisors) that debates recommendations. **They are advisors, not legal directors or corporate officers**, and their consensus is not an approval.

### 52. AI Board meeting

### 54. Natural language enterprise command

### 55. Enterprise search graph

### 56. Operational Control Tower

### 58. Bottleneck Engine

### 70. Health state
